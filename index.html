Each supervision has its own page. On it you’ll find the work to hand in, which you write just as you will in the online exam, and some short exercises in the workbench. The workbench has a microscope, the body in 3D, real recordings, an organ bath, diagrams you can run, and R. Pick a supervision below to get started.

Before you start

  • It’s best on a laptop or desktop computer with an up-to-date Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. A wide screen helps, because the instructions sit beside the workbench. It works on a tablet or a phone too, though things get a bit cramped.
  • Everything you do, including your essays, ticks, answers and files, is saved in this browser, on this computer, and all the supervision pages share your files. If you use another computer or a private window, you’ll be starting from scratch.
  • Some lab and library computers clear the browser when you log out, so keep a copy of your work. You can download everything with My files, and the work you hand in as a Word document.

How the supervisions work

  • The work to hand in is in the first tab, Supervision work. Write it as you would in the exam, typed in, with nothing pasted in from elsewhere, and without notes unless the question says you can look things up. Then submit it and hand it in from the same page, using the hand-in password I give you.
  • The instructions on the left take you through the week’s exercises. The steps tick themselves off as you go, and every question has a model answer you can look at once you’ve had a go.
  • Exercises marked Optional and sections marked If you have time are extras. The other papers in Supervision work are there for practice and revision, and only the work marked To hand in needs to be handed in.
  • New to the workbench? Its guide takes you round each tab in turn.
